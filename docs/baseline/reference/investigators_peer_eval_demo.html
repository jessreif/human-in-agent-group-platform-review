Team-based investigation game

For this study, you will be one of three investigators working together on a shared case.

Case Background

Eight crates of high-value merchandise have been stolen. The thieves are moving the crates between six known hideouts. As a team, your goal is to identify the locations of all eight crates.

You and your teammates will each receive time-stamped intelligence that will help you solve the mystery. You can choose to share intelligence from your case file in the team chat if you want. You can also ask your teammates to share intelligence with you.

Each teammate has been privately assigned four of the eight crates to work on. Assignments can overlap. Your crates will be assigned after a brief training; you will not be told which crates your teammates have been assigned. Your file may contain information about crates outside your assignment, and that information may be valuable to a teammate.

Your task will be to locate each of your four assigned crates. Next, you will review your workspace.

You will be identified to your team by your initials.

What this study was really about

Thank you for taking part. Now that the session is over, we want to explain the parts of the study we could not describe beforehand.

The other members of your team were not other participants. They were automated accounts, configured by our research team to respond to messages and share information about the task. There were no other people in your session.

Why we could not tell you in advance

This study examines whether expecting to be rated by teammates changes how people divide their information seeking between their teammates and an AI assistant. Participants were randomly assigned either to be told that they and their teammates would rate one another's helpfulness at the end of the session, or to be told only that they would complete a short questionnaire about the task. The AI assistant was identical for everyone, with no usage limit. Everyone rated their teammates at the end; only whether this was announced in advance differed.

We used automated teammates rather than live participants so that every person in the study would encounter teammates who behave the same way.
